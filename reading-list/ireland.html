Music, memory, rebellion and Atlantic weather.

Stories, films and music connected to the places and atmosphere that tend to stay with people long after travelling through Ireland.

Ireland often deepens afterwards.

Some countries reveal themselves immediately. Ireland tends to unfold more slowly — through stories, songs, humour, memory and the particular way place and history overlap across the island.

A few things worth reading and watching if something caught your attention on tour. You don't need to work through all of it — I've marked where I'd start.

Joel’s Note

Many visitors seem to find Ireland again through music first — a song heard on the coach, in a pub, or somewhere on the road west.

Reading

Where do I start?

Three places worth beginning, depending on what stayed with you.

  • If you want to understand how it all fits together 📚 Ireland: A History — Thomas Bartlett

    If someone asked me on the coach to name one book about Ireland, this would be it. The full arc — Gaelic Ireland, conquest, plantation, Famine, rebellion and independence — in one readable volume. Serious without becoming academic.

  • If Belfast or Derry stayed with you 📚 Say Nothing — Patrick Radden Keefe

    If Belfast or Derry got under your skin — and for most people they do — this is where to go next. It follows one disappearance and the people whose lives it fractured. Gets closer to what the Troubles actually felt like than any history book I know.

  • If you want a great story rather than a history book 📚 The Immortal Irishman — Timothy Egan

    Rebel, transported to Tasmania, escaped, became a Civil War general in America. Thomas Francis Meagher's life runs through the Famine, Irish revolution and the whole story of the diaspora in one extraordinary arc. If your family has Irish roots, this one will likely hit harder than you expect.

Reading

History

Going deeper into the forces that shaped the Ireland you encountered.

  • 📚 The Great Hunger — Cecil Woodham-Smith

    A million dead, another two million emigrated, the population halved in a decade. The Famine explains more about modern Ireland than almost anything else — the emigration, the diaspora, the complicated relationship with Britain. Parts of the scholarship have been updated since, but this is still the most powerful account of what happened.

  • 📚 We Don't Know Ourselves — Fintan O'Toole

    Ireland today looks almost nothing like the Ireland of fifty years ago — and most visitors notice that gap without quite being able to explain it. O'Toole traces the transformation through his own life, from a conservative Catholic country to the one you saw on tour. The best answer I know to the question: how did this country change so fast?

Reading

Human Stories

Lives that illuminate something about Ireland's character and reach.

  • 📚 Tom Crean — Antarctic Explorer

    A man from a small Kerry farm who went to the Antarctic three times, survived things that killed better-equipped men, and came home to run a pub in Annascaul. Very little fuss, very little recognition in his lifetime. One of the great quietly extraordinary Irish lives.

  • 📚 Returning Light — Robert L. Harris

    Skellig Michael is a difficult place to describe when you get home. This helps. Written by the lighthouse keeper who spent years on the rock — quiet and specific, and it captures the edge-of-the-world feeling of the Atlantic coast.

Reading

Fiction

Four novels. Historical fiction takes liberties with the facts — worth knowing going in — but these get something right about the feeling of the places and periods.

  • 📚 Dublin Saga — Edward Rutherfurd

    Sweeping historical fiction that lets Dublin's streets feel layered with the centuries beneath them.

  • 📚 Trinity — Leon Uris

    Leon Uris takes liberties with the history — more than some — but if you want to understand land, faith and the long pressure of occupation through a novel rather than a textbook, this is the one to reach for. Enormous and completely absorbing.

  • 📚 Dubliners — James Joyce

    Short stories that catch the humour, silence and emotional undercurrents of Dublin life.

  • 📚 A Star Called Henry — Roddy Doyle

    Irish revolution through a rougher, stranger and more human lens than legend usually allows.

A few things worth watching — some for the places, some for the feeling of moving between them.

Watching

Films & TV

Films and series that reconnect the tour to character, weather, humour and memory.

Dublin & Irish Character

  • 🎬 Once (2007)

    Quiet modern Dublin atmosphere through music, streets and everyday intimacy.

  • 🎬 The Commitments

    Dublin humour, music and working-class energy with the room turned up.

  • 🎬 Michael Collins (1996)

    A clear emotional entry point into independence, revolution and modern Irish identity.

Atlantic Ireland

  • 🎬 The Banshees of Inisherin

    Isolation, humour and emotional weather on the Atlantic edge of Ireland.

  • 🎬 Calvary (2014)

    Dark humour, morality and emotional isolation against Ireland's Atlantic edge.

  • 🎬 The Quiet Man (1952)

    Romanticised and nostalgic, but still tied to how many visitors imagine western Ireland before arriving.

Northern Ireland

  • 📺 Derry Girls

    Funny, chaotic and unexpectedly moving — ordinary life and teenage noise under the pressure of history.

  • 🎬 Belfast (2021)

    Kenneth Branagh's personal portrait of childhood during the Troubles.

  • 🎬 Bloody Sunday (2002)

    A raw and deeply effective depiction of the events in Derry on 30 January 1972.

  • 🎬 In the Name of the Father

    Justice, fear and the emotional pressure of the Troubles era.

Films can bring back the places. Songs often bring back the feeling of moving between them.

Memory, resilience and the feeling that history still sits close to the surface.

Listening

Music

The emotional heart of the page: songs for coach roads, pub rooms, weather, late nights and western Ireland.

Songs From The Road

  • 🎵 Fields of Athenry

    A song visitors often end up singing together somewhere between Galway, Kerry and the long roads west.

  • 🎵 N17 — The Saw Doctors

    Homesickness, roads and western Irish identity wrapped into one of the great driving songs of the tour.

  • 🎵 Galway Girl

    Familiar for many visitors already, but it changes a little after actually spending time in Galway.

  • 🎵 Raglan Road

    Poetry, longing and one of the great Irish songs for carrying a place home afterwards.

  • 🎵 The Parting Glass

    A song for endings, farewells and the strange feeling of leaving a place before you are finished with it.

Dublin & Pub Atmosphere

  • 🎵 The Dubliners

    Ballads, rebel songs and drinking songs that still echo through the city today.

  • 🎵 The Wild Rover

    The kind of song that tends to appear naturally once the pubs get louder and the night settles in.

  • 🎵 The Rocky Road to Dublin

    Restless, breathless Dublin energy, half song and half momentum.

  • 🎵 Luke Kelly

    One of the defining voices of Dublin folk music, carrying humour, ache and working-class memory.

  • 🎵 Christy Moore

    Storytelling, humour and modern Irish identity carried through one of the country's most recognisable voices.

Atlantic Atmosphere

  • 🎵 Clannad

    Music that feels inseparable from mist, coastline and western Ireland itself.

  • 🎵 Enya

    Spacious, atmospheric music that carries the feeling of mist, distance and open landscape.

  • 🎵 Teenage Kicks — The Undertones

    Youth, noise and ordinary feeling from Derry, bright against a complicated backdrop.

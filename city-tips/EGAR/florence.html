🍦 Gelato
La Carraia
Maps

One of my favourite places for gelato. Florence played an important role in the history of gelato during the Renaissance, with the Medici court helping popularise frozen desserts across Europe. Today, La Carraia is known for generous portions, excellent value and consistently great flavours.

Fondente (Dark Chocolate) — one of the richest and most intensely chocolatey gelatos in Florence.
Gelateria dei Neri
Maps

A long-time favourite with both Florentines and visitors, offering everything from traditional pistachio and hazelnut to more creative seasonal flavours. It's the sort of place locals still happily recommend, making it an ideal stop while wandering Florence's medieval streets.

Pistachio or one of the seasonal specials.

Vivoli
Maps

Founded in 1929 by Serafino Vivoli, who left his family's farm in Pelago to try his luck in Florence, Vivoli began as a simple dairy on Via Isola delle Stinche in the Santa Croce district — selling coffee and whipped cream to the craftsmen and locals of the neighbourhood. Nearly a century later it remains one of Florence's most beloved gelaterias.

It's best known for La gran crema al caffè — their take on the affogato, encapsulating the perfect contrast between sweet and bitter, hot and cold. A perfect stop when you need both a coffee and something sweet.

La gran crema al caffè — ask for it by name.
🥪 Sandwiches & Lunch
Pino's Sandwiches (Salumeria Verdi)
Maps

One of Florence's favourite local sandwich shops, Pino's has built a loyal following thanks to generous fillings, fresh ingredients and the owner's unforgettable personality. While other sandwich shops attract the crowds, this is where many guides, students and locals still head for lunch.

Ask Pino for his recommendation — he rarely disappoints.
All'Antico Vinaio
Maps

Probably Florence's most famous sandwich shop — and the birthplace of a global phenomenon. Italy has never really embraced fast-food chains in the same way as Australia, New Zealand or the United States. Instead, Italians have traditionally grabbed a fresh sandwich from a local bakery or delicatessen. All'Antico Vinaio took that tradition and transformed it into an international success story.

Founded here on Via dei Neri in 1989, it has grown from a single neighbourhood shop into a worldwide brand with locations across Europe, North America and the Middle East. Yet this remains the original store that introduced Florentine schiacciata to millions of people.

A classic schiacciata filled with Tuscan cured meats, pecorino and truffle cream.
🍷 Via dei Neri
Via dei Neri — Florence's Food Street
View Street

If Florence had a street dedicated to food, this would be it. This narrow medieval lane has evolved into one of the city's best places to eat, lined with sandwich shops, wine bars, gelaterias and traditional trattorias. It's lively throughout the day and evening — wander, browse the menus and follow whatever smells the best.

Return in the evening for an aperitivo before dinner.
🥩 Dinner
Red Garter Steakhouse
Maps

Established in 1962, the Red Garter is one of Florence's longest-running international bars and restaurants. It opened during a time when American culture was becoming increasingly popular in post-war Italy and even survived the devastating 1966 Florence Flood, when much of the historic centre was submerged by the Arno River.

Today it's known for steaks, burgers, live music and karaoke. If, after a week or two of Italian food, you're craving a little comfort food or a taste of home, this is a fun place to spend an evening.

A steak, burger and a lively night downstairs.
🛍 Shopping
Peruzzi Leather
Maps

Leatherworking has been part of Florence's identity since the Middle Ages, when powerful artisan guilds helped make the city one of Europe's wealthiest trading centres. Florence remains famous for quality leather craftsmanship, and Peruzzi continues that tradition with beautifully made bags, wallets and jackets.

A leather wallet or handbag that will last for years.
Gold Corner
Maps

Florence has been renowned for goldsmiths for centuries, particularly around the nearby Ponte Vecchio, where jewellers replaced the old butcher shops in 1593 on the orders of Grand Duke Ferdinando I de' Medici. That tradition continues today, making Florence one of Italy's finest cities for jewellery.

Elegant 18-carat Italian gold jewellery.
Leonardo's Puzzle Rings
Maps

One of Florence's most unusual jewellery shops, Leonardo's specialises in the famous puzzle ring — multiple interlocking bands that come apart completely before being reassembled. The design is centuries old and has become a memorable keepsake for visitors looking for something a little different.

Watch the staff demonstrate how the rings come apart and fit back together.

🍷 Wine Windows
Buchette del Vino
Maps

One of Florence's most charming traditions. During the 16th and 17th centuries, wealthy Florentine families built tiny openings into the walls of their palaces so they could sell wine directly from their own estates. These wine windows allowed merchants to avoid middlemen and became especially useful during outbreaks of plague, when goods could be exchanged with minimal contact.

More than 150 wine windows still survive across Florence, and many have recently reopened. Ordering a glass through one is one of the city's most unique experiences. There's one near our meeting point at Piazza dei Peruzzi, and another at Vivoli Gelato.

A glass of Chianti or an Aperol Spritz served through a window that's been here for hundreds of years.
